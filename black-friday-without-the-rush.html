Black Friday, Without the Rush

By Lexi · Lifestyle

The morning after Thanksgiving, I like to pour coffee before I open a single sale email. There’s usually a pie plate in the fridge with one sliver left and a sweater draped over a chair that belongs to somebody else. The house is somewhere between hosting and being mine again. I’m in no particular hurry to move it along.

Black Friday can be a good day to shop. If the moisturizer you’re about to finish is finally on sale, lovely. The same goes for the winter boots you’ve been putting off replacing or the sheets you’ve been watching for a month. I just like to decide what I need while my coffee is still hot and the countdown clocks are still somebody else’s problem.

Start with what you already use

Before I look at the offers, I take a quick inventory. How much sunscreen is left? Do I already have an unopened cleanser? Are the guest towels actually worn out, or did I just see some very persuasive photos of towels?

From there, I make a short list. I’ll replace an essential that’s running low, buy something I’ve already planned for, or pick up a gift when I have a specific person and a specific gift in mind. I’m happy to buy a spare of a product I use steadily, as long as I’ll get through it before it expires. My bathroom cabinet has enough things in it that seemed like a good idea at the time.

Compare the price you’ll actually pay

Look at the final total with shipping, check the size or quantity, and compare it with the price you’d usually pay. A big percentage on a smaller bottle can be less appealing once you do the math. And if a sale item is final sale, I’m particularly careful about buying a shade or size I’ve never tried.

If you came for a $20 item and the cart is now at $48 because free shipping starts at $50, go back to the original item and see what shipping costs. Sometimes paying for delivery is the cheaper option. I find this especially annoying because it’s true.

Let the weekend end at its own pace

If family is heading home, offer coffee for the drive and pack up a little pie if there’s any left. I like to see people out without trying to get the kitchen back in order at the same time. The dishes will be there when the car pulls away. They’re very dependable that way.

If people are staying, lunch can be leftovers or a cheese sandwich. Take a walk if everyone wants one. If somebody would rather read on the couch, let them. A day after hosting, it’s lovely to spend time with people without having to produce another occasion.

I’ll do my shopping in one short sitting, close the tabs, and see who’s still around for coffee. If I found a better price on something I was going to buy anyway, that’s a good Black Friday. If I bought nothing and somebody stayed long enough for one more slice of pie, that’s a pretty good Friday too.
