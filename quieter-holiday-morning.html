A Quieter Holiday Morning

By Lexi · Wellness

The morning I’m hosting dinner, I usually think about the oven before my feet touch the floor. Then I remember the towels in the dryer and the text I still owe my sister. By the time the toast pops, I’ve already spent half the morning in my head.

So I’ve started keeping a few minutes for myself before I get to any of it. Some mornings I step outside. Other mornings I sit down and eat my toast while it’s still warm.

Step outside for a minute

I pull a sweater over whatever I slept in and stand on the doorstep. Sometimes the neighbor is out with his dog. Sometimes all I hear is the little click the door makes behind me. A minute is usually enough for me to notice what kind of morning it is.

If it’s raining or the house is already busy, I stand by the kitchen window while the toast browns. There’s usually something to see, even if it’s just the rain collecting on the railing.

Sit down for breakfast

Butter the toast. Cut up an orange. Put your phone somewhere you can’t reach it without standing up, and give yourself the length of breakfast before you check it.

I used to eat standing at the counter on busy mornings, opening drawers between bites. Sitting down takes about the same amount of time. I enjoy the toast more, and I usually remember to finish the orange.

Let one thing wait

Look at what you’ve planned and pick one thing that can happen later. If people are coming for dinner, you might need to clear the table, but you probably don’t need to fold the towels first. Shut the dryer door and get back to them when you have a minute.

If you only have five minutes

Take one minute at the door or window, three to sit down with whatever breakfast you have, and one to decide what can wait. If you only get to the breakfast part, enjoy it. The rest of the day will give you plenty to do.
